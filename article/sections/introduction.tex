\section{Introduction}

%--------------------------------------------------
% 1. Contexte général
%--------------------------------------------------

Many physical, environmental and climate systems are subject to uncertainty arising from incomplete 
information, measurement errors, and random external perturbations. Such uncertainties can be 
incorporated into mathematical models through stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs).

%--------------------------------------------------
% 2. Revue de littérature
%--------------------------------------------------

The mathematical analysis of stochastic partial differential equations has received considerable 
attention over the last decades; see, for example, \cite{DaPrato2014,Flandoli1995,Lord2014}. Optimal 
control problems governed by partial differential equations constitute a classical research area in 
applied mathematics and engineering \cite{Lions1971,Hinze2009,Gunzburger2003}. The combination of 
stochastic dynamics and PDE-constrained optimization has generated a rapidly growing field motivated 
by applications in environmental sciences, fluid mechanics, groundwater management, and climate modeling.

%--------------------------------------------------
% 3. Motivation de l'étude
%--------------------------------------------------

Among the most important models in environmental sciences are advection--diffusion equations, which 
describe the transport of pollutants, heat, humidity, and chemical species in fluids and porous media. 
When uncertainties are taken into account, stochastic advection--diffusion equations provide a more 
realistic description of the underlying physical processes.

Optimal control of SPDEs has attracted significant attention due to its numerous applications in
engineering, environmental management, and climate science.

The purpose of this work is to study an optimal control problem governed by a stochastic 
advection--diffusion equation driven by additive noise. We establish the well-posedness of the state 
equation, prove the existence of optimal controls, derive the corresponding adjoint equation, and 
obtain first-order optimality conditions. A finite element approximation is also proposed and implemented 
using FEniCSx.

%--------------------------------------------------
% 4. Contributions
%--------------------------------------------------

The main contributions of this work can be summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item We establish a variational formulation for a stochastic advection--diffusion equation driven 
by additive noise.
\item We prove the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions.
\item We formulate and analyze an optimal control problem governed by the stochastic state equation.
\item We derive the corresponding adjoint equation and first-order optimality conditions.
\item We propose a finite element discretization suitable for implementation in FEniCSx.
\item We illustrate the theoretical developments through numerical experiments relevant to environmental 
and climate applications.
\end{itemize}

%--------------------------------------------------
% 5. Organisation
%--------------------------------------------------

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the mathematical model. 
Section 3 presents the variational formulation. Existence and uniqueness results are established 
in Section 4. The optimal control problem is formulated in Section 5. The adjoint equation and 
optimality conditions are derived in Sections 6 and 7. A finite element discretization is presented 
in Section 8. Numerical experiments are reported in Section 9, followed by concluding remarks.
